Model Council Meta-Analysis

What each model brought to the table

Claude — Anthropic
"Urgent, data-driven disruption narrative"
  • Pyramid → obelisk: Consulting firms are structurally transforming. Fewer layers. More leverage per person.
  • Specific metrics: 15M paid Copilot seats vs. 33M active users — a significant adoption gap that matters for licensing strategy.
  • Concrete restructuring evidence: Big Four named, numbers cited. PwC, EY, Deloitte, KPMG all moving now.
  • Multi-agent orchestration positioned as the frontier — limited supply, growing demand, early mover advantage.
  • Failure rate emphasis: Led with 95% GenAI ROI failure to set realistic client expectations as a differentiator.
GPT — OpenAI
"Steady embrace-and-guide stance"
  • Citizen dev as expansion: Where Claude saw commoditization, GPT saw growth — businesses need governance, fusion teams, CoE setup.
  • Partnership framing: Focus on co-creating with clients in fusion teams rather than replacing junior work with AI.
  • Pragmatic value focus: Stressed asking "why?" before "how?" — validate business cases, not just deploy features.
  • Specialization over breadth: Double down on Power Platform/D365 CRM strengths; partner for adjacent capabilities.
  • Tone: Technology as enabler requiring guidance. Strengthen long-term client relationships over disruption fears.

Bottom Line

The practice that thrives vs. the one that struggles

Thrives in 2026

  • Invests in senior talent — Power Platform + D365 + AI combo, not junior headcount
  • Leads with governance & data readiness — not shiny AI demos
  • Builds repeatable agent solutions for common business processes
  • Offers outcome-based engagements that prove ROI with clear KPIs
  • Specializes deeply in 2–3 industries or solution areas
  • Treats Copilot/agents as a platform ecosystem, not a feature

Struggles in 2026

  • Competes on price for commodity implementation work
  • Lacks governance expertise as clients increasingly demand it
  • Can't retain talent in a 15–22% annual salary inflation market
  • Sells "AI strategy" without delivery proof or measurable outcomes
  • Operates as a generalist with no defensible differentiation
The market is bifurcating. High-value strategic work — governance, architecture, outcome delivery — commands growing premiums. Routine configuration work faces AI commoditization and pricing pressure. There is no comfortable middle ground. The interesting disagreement between the models: Claude says act urgently, GPT says guide steadily. Both are probably right at the same time, depending on which client you're talking to.
