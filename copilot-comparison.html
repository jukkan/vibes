Adoption: Organic Pull vs. Enterprise Push

GitHub Copilot

Developers choose it

81% of developers install the extension the same day they get access. 96% start accepting suggestions immediately. Users report feeling "cognitively incomplete" without it.

Bottom-up adoption — individuals and teams adopt, then enterprises follow. 90% of Fortune 100 have deployed it.

M365 Copilot

Employers mandate it

When Copilot is the only AI tool provided, 68% adopt it. When ChatGPT is also available, Copilot drops to 18%. With all three major platforms available, only 8% choose Copilot.

Top-down distribution — executives buy licenses, then struggle to prove ROI. Some companies use only 10% of paid seats.

At list price ($30/seat/month), 15M M365 Copilot seats would yield $5.4B/year. Analyst estimates suggest actual revenue is far lower due to heavy discounting — likely $1.3–2B annualized, implying effective prices of $7–11/seat/month.

Why Coding Works and Knowledge Work Doesn't (Yet)

Code Generation

Verifiable outputs

Code either compiles and passes tests or it doesn't. Developers get instant feedback on whether the AI suggestion was useful. Productivity gains are measurable: 55% faster task completion in controlled studies.

The task is narrow and well-defined: predict the next line, complete the function, suggest the pattern. LLMs are genuinely good at this.

Knowledge Work

Unverifiable outputs

Did the email summary miss something important? Was the meeting recap accurate? Is the document draft trustworthy? Users can't easily verify without doing the work themselves.

The task is broad and context-dependent: understand my org, my role, my data, my intent. Accuracy NPS is negative because the cost of a wrong answer in business context is high.

The core asymmetry: AI coding tools augment a task with a clear feedback loop (does the code work?). AI productivity tools attempt to replace judgment in contexts where errors are hard to detect and expensive to fix. This is why GitHub Copilot has product-market fit and M365 Copilot has a distribution strategy.

The Capex Question

Microsoft spent $88B in capex in FY2025 and is on pace for $144B+ in FY2026. The entire AI coding tools market was ~$7B in 2025. Even if Microsoft captured 100% of it, that's less than 5% of annual capex.

The gap between AI investment and AI product revenue is roughly 18–22×. Microsoft's proven AI product (GitHub Copilot) addresses a niche market. Its mass-market AI product (M365 Copilot) is being rejected by users who have alternatives.

The July 2026 M365 price increases will bundle Copilot Chat into base subscriptions, boosting "AI revenue" on paper. But bundling a product into a price increase is not the same as proving demand — it's the same playbook Microsoft used with Teams, and it tells you everything about how confident they are in organic adoption.
